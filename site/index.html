Under the hood

A straight line from camera to cursor

No cloud, no ML server, no waiting on a network. Four stages run on your Mac, and the last one speaks the same language as your mouse — so every app responds the way it always has.

The filter stage is why the pointer feels calm instead of jittery: adaptive smoothing holds steady while you aim and gets out of the way the moment you move fast.

  1. 01

    Camera frames

    The webcam's live feed, processed in memory and discarded frame by frame. Nothing is recorded, ever.

    AVCaptureSession
  2. 02

    Hand landmarks, on-device

    Apple's Vision framework finds 21 joints on your hand in every frame — entirely on your Mac's own silicon.

    VNDetectHumanHandPoseRequest
  3. 03

    1€ filter smoothing

    The One-Euro filter — a technique built for exactly this problem — kills jitter during precise moves without adding lag to fast ones.

    OneEuroFilter
  4. 04

    Real clicks, real windows

    Gestures become the same events a physical mouse produces — clicks, scrolls, drags — plus window moves through the Accessibility API.

    CGEventAXUIElement

Private by architecture

Your camera feed never leaves your Mac

Not "we don't look at it" — it physically can't go anywhere.

Processed live, thrown away

Frames go straight to Apple's on-device Vision framework and are discarded. Nothing is recorded, stored, or uploaded — ever.

No accounts, no telemetry

No sign-up, no analytics, no crash reporters phoning home. The app's only network call is the update check you trigger yourself.

Off means off

Disable it from the menu bar — or hold ✌️ at the camera — and the session stops on the spot. The menu-bar hand hollows out so you always know.

Questions

FAQ

The honest answers, including the one about the App Store.

Why isn't it on the App Store?
It can't be. AirControl moves your cursor and windows using macOS accessibility APIs, which the App Store sandbox forbids — the same reason tools like Rectangle and BetterTouchTool ship outside it. It's distributed directly, with checksums you can verify.
What does it need to run?
macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later and any built-in or external camera. It asks for two permissions on first run: Camera (to see your hand) and Accessibility (to move windows and click for you). A two-minute welcome tour walks you through both, then lets you practice every gesture safely on fake windows before anything real is touched. Older or Intel Macs get a Lightweight mode (on by default on Intel) that lowers the camera and detection load without changing how it feels.
Is it accurate enough to actually use?
That was the whole project. Deliberate hold-times, motion gates, and per-gesture thresholds mean gestures fire when you mean them and not when you don't — and every threshold is adjustable in the tuning panel if your hands disagree with the defaults. Calibration maps your comfortable range of motion to the whole desktop, so you never stretch.
Who is this for?
People eating lunch at their desk. Presenters who want to drive slides from across the room. Anyone managing RSI who wants stretches of the day without gripping a mouse. And honestly — anyone who's ever wanted to move a window by grabbing it out of the air.
Does it drain the battery?
When no hand is visible for a few minutes it drops into a low-power rest mode and the camera work nearly stops; showing your hand wakes it instantly. You can tune the idle timeout, or just turn it off from the menu bar when you're done.
